Ethiopia, located in the Horn of Africa, is one of the oldest nations in the world with a history that dates back thousands of years. 
It is known for being the only African country that was never colonized, maintaining its sovereignty throughout the Scramble for Africa. 
The country is home to ancient civilizations such as the Kingdom of Aksum, which was a major trading empire and one of the earliest adopters of Christianity.

The capital city, Addis Ababa, serves as both the political and cultural heart of Ethiopia. 
It hosts the headquarters of the African Union and numerous international organizations. 
The city is a vibrant mix of tradition and modernity, with bustling markets, historic churches, and a growing tech scene. 
Ethiopia’s diverse population speaks over 80 languages, including Amharic, Oromo, and Tigrinya, reflecting its rich cultural mosaic.

Ethiopia’s economy is largely based on agriculture, with coffee being one of its most famous exports. 
In recent years, the country has made significant strides in infrastructure development, including new roads, railways, and energy projects. 
Despite challenges such as drought and political tensions, Ethiopia continues to pursue ambitious goals for growth and regional integration, positioning itself as a key player in East Africa.
